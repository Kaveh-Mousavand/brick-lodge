Observations

on bricks in representation theory of algebras

This page collects observations on bricks — examples, counterexamples and short results that have been communicated to the Brick Lodge — with full credit to those who found them. The entries are listed in the order in which they were added, and may not yet have appeared in a refereed journal or on the arXiv. Throughout, k is an algebraically closed field, and A is a finite-dimensional associative k-algebra.

1. On infinite semibrick of pairwise extension-orthogonal rigid bricks

Example communicated by H. Enomoto, constructed with AI (OpenAI Codex, GPT-6) — October 2026 (no arXiv preprint)  ·  Note (PDF)

Setting: k is an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. A semibrick is a set of pairwise Hom-orthogonal bricks, and a brick X is rigid if Ext1A(X, X) = 0. A semibrick 𝕊 consists of pairwise extension-orthogonal rigid bricks if Ext1A(X, Y) = 0 for all X, Y ∈ 𝕊, including the case X = Y.

A finite-dimensional algebra can admit an infinite semibrick of pairwise extension-orthogonal rigid bricks.

Credit, background and motivation. This example was communicated to us by Haruhisa Enomoto, to whom we are grateful, in October 2026 (the attached note is dated October 4, 2026). Semibricks, introduced through the bijection between semibricks and wide subcategories, are at the heart of the brick-Brauer–Thrall conjectures: the Semibrick Conjecture of Enomoto (arXiv:2005.01626) predicts that an algebra is brick-infinite if and only if it admits an infinite semibrick. It is natural to ask how well-behaved such a semibrick can be made. In arXiv:2606.27063 Mousavand–Paquette asked (Question 4.2) whether, over a fixed algebra, a semibrick of pairwise extension-orthogonal rigid bricks is necessarily finite and, if so, whether its cardinality is bounded in terms of the algebra. Their Example 4.1 produces finite such semibricks of arbitrarily large cardinality, but only by changing the algebra each time, and their argument (based on τ-tilting theory) shows that an algebra with an infinite such semibrick must have global dimension at least two.

Main result. The example answers this question negatively. Over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, there is a connected basic algebra Λ with three simple modules, of dimension 21, with (rad Λ)3 = 0 and global dimension 2 (the least possible), together with modules Bn (n ≥ 0) of dimension vector (n+1, n+1, n+1) such that HomΛ(Bm, Bn) is k if m = n and 0 otherwise, and Ext1Λ(Bm, Bn) = 0 for all m, n ≥ 0. Hence {Bn} is an infinite semibrick of pairwise extension-orthogonal rigid bricks, and over this one algebra even the sizes of finite such semibricks are unbounded. Each Bn has projective dimension exactly 2, and dim Ext2(Bm, Bn) = (m+1)(n+1) − δmn.

Construction and AI provenance. The algebra Λ is an instance of the universal-localization construction of Neeman–Ranicki–Schofield (Math. Proc. Cambridge Philos. Soc. 2004), applied to the universal enveloping algebra U of the Lie algebra sl2. The finite-dimensional U-modules embed fully and exactly into mod Λ with an extension-closed image (the modules on which two distinguished arrows act invertibly), so Hom and Ext1 are preserved, and Bn is the image of the (n+1)-dimensional simple sl2-module; the required vanishing then reduces to the complete reducibility of sl2-modules in characteristic zero. The example was created by AI: according to the note, the construction and its proof were developed with OpenAI’s Codex agent in research directed by Haruhisa Enomoto, and the manuscript — a LaTeX/PDF note titled “An infinite semibrick of rigid modules”, listing “Codex (GPT-6)” as its author — was drafted with GPT-6 in Codex. It has not yet been refereed.

📎 Attached note (PDF): An infinite semibrick of rigid modules — Codex (GPT-6), October 4, 2026; shared by H. Enomoto.

Connections to other entries of the Brick Lodge:

• Open Questions, item 3 (Rigid semibricks): part (1) is answered affirmatively by this example; part (2) — whether such an algebra must be (strictly) wild — remains open. Our own remark: the quotient of Λ by the ideal generated by the idempotent of the third vertex is the path algebra of the Kronecker quiver with four arrows, which is strictly wild, so Λ is itself strictly wild; the example is therefore consistent with an affirmative answer to part (2). It also settles only the existence version of the MathOverflow question, not the question of whether every brick-infinite algebra admits such a semibrick.

• Semibrick Conjecture and 2nd bBT Conjecture: Λ is brick-infinite and admits an infinite semibrick, as predicted. The members Bn have pairwise different dimension vectors, but, being strictly wild, Λ also has infinitely many bricks of one dimension vector (arXiv:2606.27063, Proposition 3.1) — concretely, the bricks of dimension vector (1, 1, 0) given by the nonzero scalar 4-tuples on the four arrows from vertex 1 to vertex 2. Likewise, the family does not by itself witness condition (1) of the Hom-orthogonal Conjecture, which asks for pairwise Hom-orthogonal modules of the same dimension.

• Rigid-bricks Conjecture: not affected. That conjecture assumes that every brick is rigid, whereas here only the members of the semibrick are; indeed the bricks of dimension vector (1, 1, 0) above are not rigid (already over the Kronecker quotient their self-extension space is 3-dimensional, and it embeds into that over Λ).

• The example is wild and representation-infinite, so it does not bear on Questions 1 and 2 (tameness; no-gap phenomenon). We do not know of a direct implication for the remaining entries (g-vectors, Generic-brick, Stability, and Sentieri’s question).
